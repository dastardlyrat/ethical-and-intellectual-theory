# Legal Crime — ethical and legal distinction

**Author-supplied definition:** Something which is **morally or ethically repulsive and abhorrent, but legal**.

## Scope

**Legal Crime** is an author-defined critical term for a discrepancy between **legal permissibility** and **moral or ethical judgment**. Its use does **not** assert that the conduct constitutes a criminal offense, that a court has found wrongdoing, or that an established legal doctrine bears this name.

Determining whether particular conduct is legal requires the applicable jurisdiction, facts, and law at the relevant time. Moral condemnation alone does not establish a legal violation; legal permissibility alone does not establish moral or ethical approval.

This definition does not authorize retaliation, violence, unlawful investigation, or any otherwise prohibited conduct.

## Relation to other research terminology

This term is **separate** from the [witness-failure classifications](../witness-failure-terminology/README.md) (Murdered Witness, Dead Witness, Undead Witness, and Suicide), which diagnose support and contradiction defects in legal reasoning.
