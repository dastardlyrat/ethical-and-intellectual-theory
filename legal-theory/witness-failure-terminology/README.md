# Witness-Failure Terminology

The legal-system hardening and recursive implication research uses four **controlled diagnostic terms** for failures in the justificatory ancestry of a legal conclusion. These are research classifications, **not established legal doctrines or findings of crime**.

| Term | Research definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Murdered Witness (MW)** | An argument eliminates a necessary premise it previously relied upon but preserves the dependent conclusion without adequate independent replacement support. |
| **Dead Witness (DW)** | A later decision relies on an authority or conclusion whose necessary inherited justification has already collapsed. |
| **Undead Witness (UW)** | A materially narrowed or weakened supporting proposition continues to be used as though its former, stronger scope still applied. |
| **Suicide (SU)** | The same operative argument both requires and rejects a proposition under materially equivalent conditions, without a valid distinction or replacement. |

**Dependency rule:** failure of a supporting premise is **not** proof that the conclusion is false. Assess whether the premise was necessary and whether an independent, adequate justification survives; keep logical validity, authority, constitutional conformity, and governance judgment distinct.

**Attribution and status:** human-directed terminology and decomposition; AI-assisted summary under human instruction. Historical research terminology only; no jurisdiction-specific legal conclusion is asserted.
