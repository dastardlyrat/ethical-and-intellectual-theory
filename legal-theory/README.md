# Legal Theory

This directory is the **entry point for legal-theory research and terminology** in *Ethical & Intellectual Theory*. It groups separately documented concepts without requiring a new link on the repository homepage each time a theory is added.

The entries are **author-defined research concepts and diagnostic vocabulary**, not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal analysis, independent validation, or legal advice. Listing an entry does not establish that its thesis is legally correct or that a proposed mechanism has been implemented.

## Terminology and concepts

### Law, ethics, and adversarial conduct

- **[Legal Crime](legal-crime/README.md)** — Something morally or ethically repulsive and abhorrent, but legal. A distinction between legal permissibility and moral judgment; **not an assertion of a criminal offense**.
- **[Proper Construction Attack](proper-construction-attack/README.md)** — An attack carried through legal and lawful means. An analytical term; **not proof that a particular action is lawful, harmless, or authorized**.

### Legal reasoning and inherited justification

- **[Witness-Failure Terminology](witness-failure-terminology/README.md)** — Four diagnostic classes (**Murdered Witness, Dead Witness, Undead Witness, Suicide**) concerning premise dependence, inherited support, weakened authority, and contradiction. Research vocabulary; **not established legal doctrines**.

## Working method and evidence boundaries

The underlying legal-system research distinguishes **logical integrity**, **dependency/support integrity**, **operative legal authority**, **constitutional conformity**, **rights impact**, and **governance decisions**. A failure of supporting justification is not automatically proof that a conclusion is false; independent supporting routes must be considered.

Legal validity, moral or ethical judgment, political incentives, and technical feasibility are separate questions. All particular claims of unlawfulness, liability, or legal duty require applicable law and facts. Research classification alone creates no legal authorization or enforcement authority.

## Adding future research

Add each substantive term or theory in its **own descriptive subdirectory**, with a README identifying its definition, scope, source/attribution, evidentiary status, limits, and relationships to other entries. Link the new entry **here** under the appropriate category; leave the main repository README linked to this index rather than expanding the homepage with individual theories. Preserve the distinction between **proposed**, **supported**, **independently verified**, and **adopted** work. Unresolved terms should remain visibly unresolved rather than receiving inferred definitions.
