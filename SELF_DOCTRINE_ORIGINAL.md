# Self-Doctrine: Intellectual Methodology, Derivation, and Personal Attribution

**Classification:** Personal Research Ethics  
**Subject:** Intellectual property, computational analysis, methodological fingerprints, and archival restrictions  
**Status:** Doctrinal statement — methodology intentionally undisclosed

## I. Foundational Principle

The publication of source code, algorithms, or intellectual work does not constitute unrestricted ethical consent to extract, reconstruct, profile, or archive the creator's characteristic methods of operation.

A distinction must be maintained between the study of a computational work and the systematic extraction of its creator's intellectual identity.

The former may be legitimate research. The latter introduces a separate ethical boundary, irrespective of the work's licensing status.

## II. Progressive Boundary Violation

The severity of an ethical violation increases as the following conditions accumulate:

1. **Boundary transgression:** An individual's expressly communicated restrictions or wishes concerning the examination of their work are disregarded.
2. **Methodological fingerprint extraction:** Pattern analysis is used to reconstruct or characterize an individual's distinctive intellectual methods, recurring decisions, or characteristic mode of operation (M.O.).
3. **Conceptual appropriation:** Subsequent work is substantially derived from the original source or its extracted methodological characteristics without transparent attribution of the originating contribution or a clearly distinguishable independent contribution.
4. **Persistent archival:** The extracted fingerprint is retained, indexed, correlated, transferred, or made available for future reconstruction, imitation, attribution, or exploitation.

These conditions can operate independently. Their combination constitutes a progressively more serious ethical breach.

In particular, attribution of the source does not automatically justify fingerprint extraction, and authorization to inspect source code does not automatically authorize profiling its author.

## III. Personal Intellectual Property

For the purposes of this doctrine, an individual's distinctive intellectual methodology is recognized as an interest deserving protection independently of the ordinary licensing status of the works through which it becomes observable.

The systematic archival of an individual's M.O. is treated as an intrusion into personal intellectual property.

This is an ethical classification. It does not presume that every underlying characteristic is independently protected under existing intellectual-property law.

## IV. The Genetic Information Analogy

The ethical concern is analogous to extracting and archiving an individual's genetic information.

The availability of biological material does not, by itself, establish unlimited ethical permission to derive, retain, or exploit an individual's identifying information.

Likewise, the availability of source code does not, by itself, constitute permission to construct and preserve a reproducible profile of its author's intellectual characteristics.

The analogy concerns autonomy, informational boundaries, consent, attribution, and potential exploitation. It does not assert that source code and genetic information are legally or scientifically identical.

## V. Permissible Research Boundary

This doctrine does not prohibit legitimate investigation of computational problems, publicly established mathematical principles, functional requirements, algorithms, or alternative implementations.

It distinguishes:

- Understanding a problem from extracting a person's characteristic problem-solving identity.
- Studying an implementation from profiling its author.
- Independent conceptual derivation from unacknowledged appropriation.
- Functional pattern recognition from identifying methodological fingerprint extraction.

The research objective, the information extracted, the permissions involved, and the intended retention or use must all be considered.

## VI. Archival Prohibition

No research necessity, analytical convenience, permissive source-code license, or technological capability shall independently justify the archival of an individual's identifying methodological fingerprint.

The prohibited object is not ordinary computational knowledge but the reconstructed, attributable intellectual profile of an individual.

This doctrine intentionally does not disclose procedures for producing such fingerprints.

## VII. Final Axiom

**The freedom to study a work is not equivalent to the freedom to appropriate, reproduce, or archive the intellectual identity of its creator.**

The more extensively a work crosses expressed boundaries, depends upon extracted methodological fingerprints, and obscures its conceptual debt to an original contributor, the greater the ethical violation.

A research process must be evaluated not only by what it produces, but by what it extracts, whose intellectual characteristics it captures, and what it preserves for future use.
