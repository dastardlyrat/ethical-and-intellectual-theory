1. Don't use AI — and why

An LLM is not proof of correctness, lawful use, provenance, independent derivation, or another person's intent. Prefer human-directed specifications and deterministic tools when possible.

  • Do not substitute confident model explanations for compiler diagnostics, mathematical checks, and reproducible tests.
  • Do not submit unauthorized source, personal data, logs, or conversations to a model.
  • Public availability is not blanket consent to profile an author or archive identifying methods.
  • Respect the stated boundaries of creators; a refusal ends the proposed inquiry.
  • Avoid unnecessary computation when a smaller specialized tool already suffices.

2. Do this instead

  1. Define the research question, numerical requirements, constraints, and error conditions.
  2. Study authorized shading theory, HLSL and ReShade FX documentation, and independent specifications.
  3. Use permitted compilers, linters, profilers, numerical references, differential tests, and human review.
  4. To study development itself, create and document your own experimental shaders.
  5. Record sources, permissions, revisions, failures, corrections, and uncertainty.

3. If you use AI — this research program's objections

We object to unnecessary AI dependence, unauthorized disclosure, hidden conceptual debt, unverified claims, and extraction or archival of author-identifying methodological fingerprints without specific permission. These are this project's positions, not ReShade community consensus.

4. Decomposting ≠ extractive mining

In this proposal, mining is the unauthorized extraction of a work or creator's methods for advantage. Decomposting instead seeks regenerative, provenance-aware study:

Decompose → Study → Reason → Learn → Respect provenance → Correct → Regrow.

Hermetically seal Code A → identify permissible common Pattern B → disallow Code A − Pattern B.

The subtraction names an exclusion, never a source-specific residual to calculate, preserve, or reconstruct. If Pattern B cannot be established within source boundaries, stop and use independent public theory, original fixtures, or a consenting reference.

5. Why create shaders?

The operator's research purpose is to observe shader development and its errors. Broad shading theory and narrow ReShade needs motivate human-directed experimental shaders whose revisions, failures, tests, and corrections can be documented and studied. These are experimental instruments, not necessarily competing effects.

Small interchangeable research, documentation, and verification modules are an intended complementary architecture, not a proven claim of zero performance or economic effects.

6. Your place in the ecosystem

Researchers, shader authors, critics, and users may voluntarily challenge our methods, propose alternatives, or decline entirely. No participation, endorsement, source-sharing, or permission to profile is presumed.
