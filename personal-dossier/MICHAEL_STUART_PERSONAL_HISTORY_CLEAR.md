# Michael Stuart — A Short Personal History

I am an independent programmer, systems researcher, writer, and maker. My work brings together computing, archival practice, ethics, personae, and creative construction.

I write under the name and persona Alia-June. DastardlyRat is another persona and is the result of my …; I intentionally leave its origin unstated. Sfia is a name from my childhood mythology for a giant rat. These personae are part of my creative and ethical work, but my public personal name is Michael Stuart.

Through these personae, I explore consent, attribution, provenance, responsibility, personal modeling, and the limits of automated authority. A recurring concern is keeping access separate from permission, implementation separate from truth, and preservation separate from authority.

I began teaching myself computing with a TRS-80 and a tape drive. I learned BASIC by reading existing code, changing it, and observing the results. I eventually adapted parts of tabletop role-playing combat into a small computer game. Because the tape I used was old and unreliable, I sometimes lost my work and had to reconstruct it. That experience contributed to my later interest in preservation, documentation, and recoverable systems.

I later worked with DOS, batch files, early personal-computer hardware, memory configuration, modems, operating systems, and computer maintenance. In an early professional project, I built a messaging system with a Windows front end and database connected to a C++ server running on OS/2. The server communicated by modem with an alphanumeric paging service. I interpreted the available protocol documentation, diagnosed timing problems in the remote implementation, adjusted the system, and documented what worked.

My education included Latin translation, ethics, philosophy, comparative religion, community college, and university study. Translation taught me to preserve the source, examine individual words and their functions, interpret them in context, and explain how a conclusion was reached. That discipline continues to influence my technical and archival work.

Today I work independently on information assurance and integrity, provenance, archival organization, guarded computing, consent, and the responsible use of artificial intelligence. Some parts of this work are functioning tools. Other parts remain specifications, research notes, or ongoing investigations. I try to keep those stages distinct and avoid claiming more than the available evidence supports.

Creative work remains important to me. I build with LEGO, study mechanical and visual structures, and write. These activities help me explore composition, articulation, balance, transformation, and the relationship between an exterior form and the system that makes it work.

My goal is straightforward: preserve original sources, record how information changes, leave uncertainty visible when certainty has not been earned, and build systems that remain answerable to the people who use them.
